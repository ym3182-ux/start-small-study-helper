# Start Small Study Helper

## Original idea

I made a small browser-based study helper for learners who feel unsure how to begin studying. **When someone chooses their energy level and available time, the experience should give them a realistic, short to-do list. When they confirm that list, a focus timer should begin.**

## How to open it

Download or clone this repository, then open `index.html` in a web browser. No installation is needed.

## AI tool and selected prompts

I used Codex to help create this project. One important prompt was: “Create a small study helper for learners. Let someone choose their energy level and available time, then give them a short study plan when they click a button.” I also asked for a design that was simple enough to test, with HTML for structure, CSS for appearance, and JavaScript for the interactive choices and generated plan. Later, I asked: “How could I test whether a plan based only on time and energy is too demanding or too easy for a learner?” This led to the feedback buttons after the plan. I then asked to add a Pomodoro-like focus timer because receiving a plan alone did not help a learner keep track of a focused study session. Finally, I asked to make the plan a clickable to-do list and begin the focus timer only after the learner confirms it.

## Testing and reflection

My intention was to make starting a study session feel less overwhelming. I decided that the experience should not assume every learner has the same amount of energy or time. The program makes a design choice by offering only three energy levels and three time options, then matching each combination with a different clickable to-do list. The learner can check off items before committing to the session. After they choose “Confirm & start focus,” a Pomodoro-like timer begins and later offers a five-minute break. After a learner marks a session complete, the experience unlocks a small bonus reflection. The learner can say that the session felt like too much, just right, or not challenging enough, and receive a different next-step suggestion. Completing a session also adds one visible focus star, which stays saved in the same browser.

I tested the interaction by generating a plan, checking items in the to-do list, confirming the list, starting the focus timer, pausing and resetting it, and checking the completion feedback. I noticed that the first version of the completion button could be clicked immediately. Although it showed a positive message, it did not feel like the learner had participated in earning it. I revised the interaction so the button stays locked before and during the focus session. When the focus timer ends, the button unlocks and the learner can actively click it to mark the session complete and receive positive feedback. I also noticed that asking for the learner’s feelings immediately after they received a plan was poorly timed. I moved that question to after completion, where it appears as a bonus reflection and gives a suggestion based on the learner’s answer. During visual testing, I felt that the original page showed too many buttons and sentences at once. I simplified the language and changed the flow so the choice form disappears after a plan is made. I then added a to-do list because I wanted learners to see and confirm the exact tasks before committing to a focus session. Finally, I found that the bonus reflection was not visible enough, so I added a visible focus-star counter and a “✨ +1 focus star” message after each completed session. The learner sees only the current plan and timer, with a small “Change plan” option if needed. AI helped turn my ideas into HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, but I decided the audience, the time and energy choices, the order in which feedback appears, and the amount of information shown at each stage. One remaining uncertainty is whether a 5-, 15-, or 30-minute timer is the best fit for every learner, so I would want to test the tool with other students and ask what they would change.
